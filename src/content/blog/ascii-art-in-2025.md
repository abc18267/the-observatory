We have WebGL. We have SVG. We have CSS animations that would make a motion designer cry. And yet, the most interesting thing on some websites is a block of fixed-width text.

ASCII art isn't dead. It's a design choice.

## Why Terminals Feel Different

Terminals feel honest. No rounded corners. No gradient overlays. No design system. Just text on a dark background. There's a rawness to it that no amount of polish can replicate.

When you see a terminal interface on a website, your brain shifts gears. You stop scrolling and start reading. You stop consuming and start interacting. That shift is powerful.

## The Aesthetic

Green text on black. It's a cliche, and it works. The color scheme comes from old phosphor monitors. CRTs that burned images into your retinas if you stared too long. That's real history behind a CSS color.

```css
.terminal {
  background: #0a0a0a;
  color: #00ff41;
  font-family: 'JetBrains Mono', monospace;
}
```

Three lines of CSS and your website feels like a different universe.

## Building a Terminal Component

I built a terminal into this site. It opens with the Konami code (up, up, down, down, left, right, left, right, B, A). It has 20+ commands. Some give you information. Some are jokes. Some unlock hidden content.

The hard part isn't rendering text. It's making the commands feel alive. Every command should have personality. `help` should be useful. `sudo` should be funny. `void` should be unsettling.

## When to Use Terminal Aesthetics

Not everywhere. Terminal aesthetics work for hidden features, developer tools, easter eggs, and 404 pages. They don't work for navigation or primary content.

The contrast is the point. Your site looks one way. The terminal looks another way. The gap between them creates surprise. Surprise creates memory.

## The Craft of Fixed-Width Text

ASCII art is a constraint-based medium. You have characters, a monospace grid, and nothing else. No anti-aliasing. No gradients. Every curve is an approximation.

That constraint forces creativity. A coffee cup in 8 lines. A rocket ship in 12 characters wide. The limitations are the feature.

Build something with text. Ship it in a monospace font. Watch people lean in to look closer. That's the terminal effect.
